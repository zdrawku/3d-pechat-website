The manufacturer no longer makes replacement parts for that model, yet the whole appliance works perfectly – except for this one piece. Sound familiar? The solution is closer than you think: a 3D printed replacement part.

In this article we'll walk through step by step how to order a custom replacement part via 3D printing – what you need to measure and photograph, when FDM technology with PLA filament is suitable and when it isn't, how long the process takes, and exactly how an order works.

## What is 3D printing of replacement parts and why it works

3D printing builds physical objects layer by layer from a digital model. When the original manufacturer no longer offers a spare part – or charges a disproportionate price, or the delivery time is months – 3D printing offers an alternative route: creating a new part based on the broken original, precise measurements, or even just photographs.

FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) technology, which we use at 3dpechat.bg, melts plastic filament and deposits it layer by layer to build the object. It's particularly suited to functional replacement parts because:

- It allows choice of different materials according to requirements for strength, flexibility or temperature resistance
- It produces parts with good mechanical strength
- It offers fast turnaround – from a few hours to a few days
- It keeps the cost low compared to traditional small-batch manufacturing

## When FDM printing with PLA is suitable for a replacement part

PLA (polylactic acid) is the most popular 3D printing material and the main filament we use at 3dpechat.bg. It's always in stock and suitable for a wide range of applications.

### Ideal cases for PLA replacement parts:

- **Internal components of household appliances** – handles, buttons, clips, frames
- **Office and furniture** – plastic caps, adjustment elements, connecting clips
- **Children's toys** – spare parts for construction sets, wheels, mechanisms
- **Electronics** – enclosures, battery holders, project boxes
- **Decorative elements** – ornaments, frames, panels
- **Hobby projects** – parts for drones, RC models, miniatures

PLA has excellent dimensional stability, is easy to post-process and is safe for food contact (under certain conditions).

> "PLA is ideal for 90% of replacement parts around the home – stable, easy to print and inexpensive. For specialised applications there are other options too."

## When PLA is NOT suitable and what the alternatives are

Despite its versatility, PLA does have limitations. It's important to know them so you can choose the right material.

### Do not use PLA when:

- **High temperature** – PLA begins to soften at around 60°C. Not suitable for parts near motors, ovens, car interiors in sunlight
- **Outdoor use** – prolonged exposure to UV rays and moisture gradually degrades PLA
- **High impact strength required** – PLA is rigid but can fracture suddenly under heavy impact
- **Flexible parts** – hinges, membranes, seals require elastic materials

### Alternative materials (available on request):

| Material | Advantages | Typical applications |
|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| **PETG** | Good strength, impact resistance, higher thermal stability (~80°C), good clarity | Protective covers, electronics enclosures, mechanical parts under load |
| **TPU** | Flexibility, elasticity, abrasion resistance | Gaskets, dampers, flexible joints |
| **ABS** | High strength, temperature resistance, good impact toughness | Automotive parts, tool housings |

At 3dpechat.bg we work mainly with PLA, which is always in stock. Other materials such as PETG or TPU we purchase specially for your order when needed – mention this in your enquiry so we can give you an accurate quote and lead time.

## How to prepare information for an order

The quality and accuracy of the replacement part depend directly on the information you provide. Here's a detailed step-by-step guide.

### Step 1: Photos from all angles

- Take at least **4–6 clear photos** of the part from different angles
- Lighting should be good – natural light or white LED
- Include general views and close-ups of every important feature
- If the part is broken, photograph the broken pieces together (how they join)
- If possible, photograph the part next to a ruler or object with known dimensions (coin, ruler)

### Step 2: Measuring the dimensions

Precision is key. Use:

- **Callipers** (for small parts and holes) – accuracy 0.1 mm
- **Ruler or tape measure** (for larger parts)

**What to measure:**

- Overall length, width, height
- Wall thickness
- Diameter and depth of holes
- Distance between mounting holes
- Radii of rounded corners (if critical)

Record all dimensions in mm and send them as text, a table or a photo of a hand sketch with annotations.

### Step 3: Functional requirements

Describe **how the part works**:

- Does it clip? Does it rotate? Does it press?
- Is it subject to load (weight, torsion)?
- Does it have contact with water, heat, sun?
- Does it need to be transparent, flexible or particularly strong?

This information helps us choose the right material and wall thickness.

### Step 4: Sending files

If you have the **original broken part**, the best option is to send it to us (physically or via detailed photos and measurements). If you have a ready-made **3D model** (STL, 3MF, OBJ or STEP), send it directly.

You can send information by:

- **Email** – attach photos, sketches, files
- **Viber** – send photos and a brief description
- **Contact form** on the site (no file attachments – use it for initial enquiry)

## Practical tips for a successful order

### Improve accuracy with a physical sample

If possible, send us the original (even broken) part by courier. This dramatically increases the accuracy of the 3D model and reduces the need for multiple corrections.

### Allow a small tolerance

3D printing has a tolerance of around ±0.2–0.3 mm. If the part needs to fit into another (such as a lid or plug), discuss with us the need for dimensional correction.

### Consider improvements

Sometimes the original part has a design flaw that led to the breakage. During modelling we can propose reinforcement of critical areas or small changes for longer life.

### Order a small batch

If the part is prone to wear (for example a clip that's often opened/closed), consider ordering 2–3 pieces at once. The price per gramme doesn't change, and you save time and delivery costs in future.

## Limitations and expectations

### Maximum size

Our printer can print parts up to **25 × 25 × 25 cm** in one piece. Larger objects we make in parts and glue together.

### Resolution and surface quality

FDM technology prints in layers 0.1–0.3 mm thick. The surface has a characteristic "layered" effect – it's visible but doesn't interfere with functionality. For requirements for an ultra-smooth surface, post-processing is needed.

### Mechanical strength

3D printed parts are strong enough for most household applications, but are not identical to injection-moulded factory parts. It's important to discuss the expected load.

### Colour and aesthetics

PLA filament is available in many colours. Specify the desired colour in your enquiry – if we don't have the exact shade in stock, we can order it or suggest a close match.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to make a replacement part with 3D printing?

The price depends on two factors: 3D modelling (if you don't have a ready file) and the print itself. Modelling is determined by complexity and time to produce – you learn the price with the quote. Printing is calculated by filament weight – between €0.80 and €1.50 per 10 g, depending on the tariff. The minimum order is €3. For a typical small replacement part (20–50 g) the total price is often between €5 and €15.

### Can I just send a photo of the broken part?

Yes, you can. Photos from all angles, together with basic dimensions, are often sufficient for modelling a simple part. For more complex shapes it may be necessary to send the physical sample by courier or provide additional sketches and descriptions.

### How long does production take?

Small replacement parts are usually ready in 1–3 working days after model approval. Larger parts or small batches – 3–7 working days. If it's urgent and the schedule allows, we can prioritise the order for a 30% surcharge.

### Will the 3D printed part last as long as the original?

In most cases – yes. FDM printing with PLA creates functional parts with good mechanical strength. For specific conditions (high temperature, outdoor use, flexibility) we can use a more suitable material such as PETG or TPU. It's important to describe how the part will be used, so we can choose the right filament and construction.

### Can you improve the original design of the part?

Yes, we often propose slight improvements – reinforcement of critical areas, thicker walls, additional ribs for strength. If the original part had a durability problem, we can adapt it to last longer.
