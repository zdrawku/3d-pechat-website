# 3D Printed Replacement Parts: How to Order a Discontinued Part
**Reading time: 9 min**

A washing machine knob, the clasp on a pushchair, a gear from a food processor, a fridge shelf bracket. These small plastic parts break more often than anything else. When the manufacturer no longer sells them, the whole appliance looks ready for the bin. Often it doesn't have to be. With **3D printing**, a missing part can be recreated from the broken original, from a photo or from measurements, and made within a few working days.

In this article you will learn what to measure and photograph, when **FDM 3D printing** in PLA is a good fit and when it is not, how long it all takes, and how an order works with us.

## Why a discontinued part is not a lost cause

Manufacturers stop making parts for old models, and sometimes they never sold them separately at all. That leaves you buying a new appliance because of a piece of plastic weighing a few grams. A 3D printer solves exactly this problem: no moulds, no minimum runs, no warehouse stock. All you need is a digital model of the part, and after that printing a single copy is perfectly normal.

The parts most often restored with 3D printing are:

- knobs, caps and buttons for appliances;
- clips, clamps and holders;
- small gears and guides (under light load);
- battery compartment covers;
- decorative and structural pieces for furniture, toys and accessories;
- adapters and connectors made to given dimensions.

## Three ways to start: a sample, a photo or measurements

You don't need to be an engineer or have a ready file. An order with us can start from a finished 3D model, a sketch, a photo or just an idea. For replacement parts, three approaches work best.

### A broken sample

This is the best option. Even if the part is in two or three pieces, they show the shape, thicknesses, holes and joints. Keep every piece, including the smallest ones, and don't clean them with harsh chemicals. If you can, also send us the part it connects to (for example the socket or the shaft) so we can check the fit.

### A photo

A photo is enough for a first assessment, but on its own it rarely gives accurate dimensions. Combine it with at least a few measured sizes. Take photos:

- from at least three sides: top, front and side;
- on a light, flat, matte background;
- with a ruler or calipers next to the part for scale;
- close up of the places where the part is fixed, clips in or rotates;
- of the spot in the appliance where the part sits, including the neighbouring parts.

### Measurements and a sketch

If there is no sample and the part is simple, you can send us a sketch with dimensions. The drawing doesn't need to be pretty. What matters is that the main values are clear and that it is marked which diameter is inner and which is outer.

## What to measure

The best tool is a pair of calipers. If you don't have any, a ruler or a paper strip will do, though with less accuracy. Measure and write down:

1. **Overall length, width and height** of the part.
2. **Hole and shaft diameters**, both inner and outer.
3. **Wall thickness** at the thinnest and thickest points.
4. **Distance between holes**, centre to centre.
5. **Threads, edges and grooves**: count, pitch, width and depth, as far as possible.
6. **Fit tolerances**: is there some play, or does the part go in with pressure?

> **Tip:** Measure in several places and write down every number. Plastic is often slightly warped by heat or age, and a single measurement from one spot can mislead you.

If you're not sure what matters, that's fine. Consultation is free and we will tell you which dimensions are critical for your case.

## When FDM printing in PLA is a good fit

We print only with **FDM technology** on a Bambu Lab A1 **3D printer**. Our main material is PLA, and it is the only one we keep in stock. PLA is easy to print, relatively stiff and holds dimensions well, which makes it suitable for many household parts.

PLA is a good choice when the part:

- is not exposed to prolonged high heat;
- doesn't carry heavy loads or take impacts;
- stays indoors, in a home, office, cupboard or room;
- is decorative, a locating piece or only lightly functional;
- is a prototype you want to test for fit before deciding on something else.

For the differences between PLA, PLA+ and other types, read [PLA, PLA+, PLA Pro and PLN: Differences and How to Choose](/en/blog/pla-vs-pla-plus-vs-pla-pro-vs-pln/).

## When PLA is not the right choice

Honesty matters, so here are the situations where PLA may be the wrong material:

| Situation | Why PLA is risky | What to consider |
|---|---|---|
| A part near a heating element or motor, or in a car parked in the sun | PLA softens at relatively low temperatures | A more heat-resistant material on request |
| Flexible seals, rubber feet, sleeves | PLA is rigid and brittle | A flexible material such as TPU on request |
| Heavily loaded gears, shafts and bearing seats | It may wear out or break | Another material or a metal part |
| Parts in contact with food or drinking water | FDM layer lines can trap dirt | We don't recommend it without further assessment |
| Safety parts (brakes, climbing buckles, locking mechanisms) | Failure can be dangerous | An original or certified part |
| Outdoor use in strong sun and frost | PLA can deform or age | PETG or another material on request |

Other materials, such as PETG or TPU, are possible on request. We buy them specially for your order, but they are not kept in stock, so arranging them may take a little longer. We will tell you about this in the quote.

If you are wondering how FDM differs from other technologies such as SLA or SLS: those are resin and powder-based processes, and we work only with FDM.

## 3D modelling: what it involves and how the price is set

When there is no ready file, the part must be modelled first. This is the real work with replacement parts: an accurate digital model is drawn from a photo, a sample and measurements. The price of 3D modelling depends on complexity, that is, on the time needed to model the part, and you learn it with the quote before we start. That way there are no surprises.

A simple part, such as a cap or a holder, is quicker to model. A part with complex geometry, many holes, chamfers and smooth curves takes more time.

If you already have a file, send it in STL, 3MF (preferred), OBJ or STEP format. Then the modelling step is not needed.

## Practical tips for a better result

- **Don't throw away the broken pieces.** They are the best source of information.
- **Describe the load.** How often the part is used, how much force it takes, whether it gets hot.
- **Consider improvements.** Sometimes the weak point of the original can be reinforced by adding thickness or a radius at the critical corner.
- **Order two copies.** For small parts, an extra copy is often cheaper than a new order later.
- **Look at the surface.** If you want a smoother finish, read [How to Smooth PLA Prints: 7 Methods That Work](/en/blog/how-to-smooth-pla-prints/).
- **Ask about strength.** Infill density affects strength, see [3D Printing Infill: Density, Strength and Filament Use](/en/blog/3d-printing-infill-density-strength/).
