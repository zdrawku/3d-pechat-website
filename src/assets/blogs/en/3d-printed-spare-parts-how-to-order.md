# 3D Printed Spare Parts: How to Order a Discontinued Part

**Reading time: 8 min**

A small plastic clip, knob, holder or gear can stop a whole appliance from working. The manufacturer no longer sells the part, the repair shop shrugs, and a new appliance costs many times more. In cases like this, **3D printing** is a practical way out. The part is made from your broken original, from a photo or from measurements, without waiting for mass production.

In this article we explain how an order works, what to measure and photograph, when **FDM 3D printing** in PLA is a good fit and when it is not, how long it takes, and how to save yourself extra back-and-forth.

## When a 3D printed spare part makes sense

3D printing is most useful when you need **a single part or a small batch**, not thousands. For quantities like that, making a mould or producing the part by traditional methods rarely pays off.

Typical cases:

- broken plastic clips, fasteners and caps;
- knobs and handles for appliances that are no longer sold;
- holders, guides and spacers;
- compartment covers (for example, battery covers);
- adapters between two incompatible parts;
- household and hobby accessories with no ready-made equivalent.

Be realistic, though: **a 3D printer does not replace a factory part in every respect.** A printed part is a good solution for many everyday needs, but not for all of them. Below you will see where the limits are.

> Before you order, ask yourself: "What load, what temperature and what kind of contact will this part have to withstand?" The answer decides whether PLA is enough.

## Four ways to start: a sample, photos, measurements or a 3D model

An order can start from a ready 3D model, a sketch, a photo or just an idea. For spare parts, these options are the most common.

### Option 1: You have the broken part

This is the easiest case. Even a broken part shows the shape, the thicknesses and where it fits. Keep **all the pieces**, including the tiny ones. Do not glue them back together before you write to us, because glue changes the dimensions and makes measuring harder.

### Option 2: You only have photos

When the part is lost or still inside the appliance, good photos and a few key measurements are often enough to judge whether it can be modelled. The clearer the photos, the fewer questions we will need to ask.

### Option 3: You have measurements and a description

If the part is missing entirely, you can tell us where it is fitted and what it has to do, and give us the dimensions of the place it sits in. The model is then created from scratch.

### Option 4: You already have a 3D model

If a model already exists (downloaded, or made by you), you can send it straight to us. We accept **STL, 3MF (preferred), OBJ and STEP**. Files are sent **by email**, because the contact form does not accept attachments.

## What to measure

The best tool is a **calliper**. If you do not have one, use a ruler, but keep in mind the accuracy will be lower. Measure:

- **overall length, width and height** of the part or of the place where it is fitted;
- **hole and pin diameters** (inside and outside);
- **wall thickness**, especially on thin features;
- **distances between holes** (centre to centre);
- **threads, teeth and profiles**: note how many there are and the pitch, if you can;
- **clearances and tolerances**: where the part must fit tightly and where it can fit loosely.

A useful tip: measure several times and write the values next to a sketch of the part. Mark which dimensions are critical, for example the diameter of a shaft the part slides onto.

### Table: what we measure and why

| What to measure | Why it matters | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Outer dimensions | Decide whether the part will fit | Measure at the widest point |
| Hole diameter | Affects fixing with screws and shafts | Measure in several places |
| Wall thickness | Affects strength | Measure at the break |
| Distance between holes | Ensures it matches the mounting | Centre to centre |
| Thread or teeth | Determines compatibility | Take a close-up and count |
| Fit tolerance | Decides whether the part fits tightly or loosely | Note where accuracy is needed |

## What to photograph

Photos often replace half of the explanation. Take:

1. **An overall shot** of the part from several sides (top, side, underneath).
2. **Close-ups** of the broken areas, threads, teeth and any surfaces with markings.
3. **A shot with a scale**: place a ruler or a coin next to the part.
4. **A photo of the mounting location**, if the part is still in the appliance, so we can see how it fits.
5. **A photo of the whole appliance** and, if there is one, of the model label.

Tips for better photos:

- use even daylight without harsh shadows;
- place the part on a light, plain background;
- avoid blurred shots and focus on the edge of the part;
- do not use filters.

> It helps to say in your email what the part does, where it is fitted and what environment it will be in: warm, damp, outdoors. These details save time.

## When FDM printing in PLA is a good choice and when it is not

We print **only with FDM technology** on a Bambu Lab A1. Our main material is **PLA**, and it is the only one we keep in stock. Other materials, such as PETG or TPU, are possible on request. We buy them specially for the order.

### When PLA is a good choice

- parts for indoor use in dry rooms;
- decorative and functional parts under light load;
- covers, holders, guides, small boxes and spacers;
- prototypes and test versions to check the fit;
- one-off parts where shape matters more than mechanical strength.

### When PLA is not the best choice

- **High temperature:** PLA softens in heat, so it is not suitable for parts near heaters or motors, or for parts left in the sun in a closed car.
- **Heavy load or impact:** thin gears, hooks and load-bearing parts can break.
- **Constant contact with water or chemicals:** in these conditions it is better to discuss another material.
- **Flexible parts:** seals and elastic parts need a flexible material such as TPU, which we order specially.
- **Safety-critical parts:** if a failure could cause injury, a printed part should not be the only protection.

### Table: choosing a material

| Situation | Suitable material | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| Dry room, low load | PLA | In stock |
| More durability and moisture | PETG | On request |
| Flexible or elastic part | TPU | On request |
| Neat look, smoother surface | PLA with post-processing | In stock (PLA) |

If you want to know more about the differences between PLA types, see [PLA, PLA+, PLA Pro and PLN: Differences and How to Choose](/en/blog/pla-vs-pla-plus-vs-pla-pro-vs-pln/). For a general overview of technologies such as FDM, SLA and SLS, look for a comparison guide of 3D printing technologies. We mention them only as general information, as we work with FDM only.

### How infill density affects strength

For functional parts, strength depends not only on the material but also on slicer settings: infill density, number of walls and print orientation. You can read more in [3D Printing Infill: Density, Strength and Filament Use](/en/blog/3d-printing-infill-density-strength/).

## How an order works, step by step

1. **Enquiry.** Write to us by email, through the contact form or on social media. Describe the part and attach photos and measurements. If you have a ready file, send it by email.
2. **Consultation.** The consultation is free. We usually reply within 24 hours. We may ask for extra photos or measurements.
3. **Quote.** The initial quote is free. If modelling is needed, its price depends on the complexity and on the time needed to model the part. You learn the price with the quote, before we start.
4. **Modelling or preparation.** If you have no ready model, one is created from the sample, photos or measurements. If needed, we can agree on a test print to check the fit.
5. **Printing.** The part is printed on an FDM 3D printer.
6. **Receiving it.** Hand-over in person or delivery by courier.

## How much does it cost

The price of printing is calculated **by weight**, according to the rate: **0.80 / 1.00 / 1.25 / 1.50 € per 10 g**. The minimum order value is **3 €**.

Small spare parts usually weigh little, so they often come close to the minimum. If modelling is needed, its price is added, and it depends on complexity. You get the exact amount with the quote. Current information is on the [Prices](/en/prices/) page.

Example: if a part weighs 20 g and the rate is 1.00 € per 10 g, the print comes to 2 €. As the minimum order is 3 €, the minimum value applies.

## Tips for a better result

- **Do not glue the broken pieces together** before you send us photos.
- **Order a test version** when the fit is critical. One small test is better than several parts that do not fit.
- **Tell us about the load and the environment**, so we can recommend a material.
- **Send a few more measurements** than you think you need.
- **If the part will be visible**, ask about surface finishing. Methods for a smooth PLA finish are described in [How to Smooth PLA Prints: 7 Methods That Work](/en/blog/how-to-smooth-pla-prints/).
- **Keep the model.** Once a part has been modelled, it can be printed again if needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a spare part be made from a photo alone?

Often yes, if the photos are clear and come with a few key measurements, such as a hole diameter and the overall length. The more accurate the information, the better the result. For more complex parts, a test print may be needed to check the fit.

### How long does it take to make a spare part?

Small parts usually take 1–3 working days, and larger parts and small batches take 3–7 working days. An urgent order is possible when the schedule allows, with a 30% surcharge. Modelling time depends on how complex the part is.

### Is PLA suitable for spare parts?

PLA suits parts under light to moderate load that are used indoors and away from heat sources. If there is higher temperature, moisture, impact or a need for flexibility, it is better to discuss another material, such as PETG or TPU, which we order specially.

### What file types can I send for a model?

We accept STL, 3MF (preferred), OBJ and STEP. Files are sent by email, because the contact form does not accept attachments.

### How much does a small spare part cost?

Printing is calculated by weight: 0.80, 1.00, 1.25 or 1.50 € per 10 g depending on the rate, and the minimum order value is 3 €. If 3D modelling is needed, its price depends on the complexity and you learn it with the quote, before we start.
