# Bulgarian Euro Coin Card: Holds the Full Set from 1 Cent to 2 Euro

**Reading time: 6 min**

On 1 January 2026 Bulgaria joined the euro area and replaced its old currency, the lev. The first Bulgarian euro coins instantly became a keepsake. Many people don't want them scattered across wallets and jars; they'd rather keep them together, as a set. That's exactly why we make **coin cards**: 3D printed holders with a slot for each of the eight coins, from 1 cent to 2 euro.

![Bulgaria-shaped coin card in the horizontal colours of the Bulgarian flag, holding a full set of euro coins](assets/real-images/bg-cards/variant-2-euro-coins.webp)

## Why it makes a great keepsake and gift

A new currency only gets its first coins once. Years from now it will be fun to show your children or grandchildren what the first Bulgarian euro set looked like: a neat collection you can hold in your hand and turn over to see both sides.

A coin card is also a gift that needs no explanation. It looks good, takes up little space and everyone understands straight away what it is.

## What it holds: the eight Bulgarian euro coins

One card holds a full set: **1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 cents, €1 and €2**. Every euro coin has a common side, shared across the euro area, and a national side. On the Bulgarian euro coins, the national side carries familiar symbols that the Bulgarian National Bank (BNB) carried over from the lev coins:

| Coin | Diameter | Design on the Bulgarian side |
|------|----------|------------------------------|
| 1 cent | 16.25 mm | Madara Rider |
| 2 cents | 18.75 mm | Madara Rider |
| 5 cents | 21.25 mm | Madara Rider |
| 10 cents | 19.75 mm | Madara Rider |
| 20 cents | 22.25 mm | Madara Rider |
| 50 cents | 24.25 mm | Madara Rider |
| €1 | 23.25 mm | St Ivan of Rila |
| €2 | 25.75 mm | Paisius of Hilendar |

The Madara Rider is an early medieval rock relief in north-eastern Bulgaria, St Ivan of Rila is the hermit saint who founded the Rila Monastery, and Paisius of Hilendar is the 18th-century monk whose history of the Bulgarians marked the start of the Bulgarian National Revival.

Notice that the sizes don't grow in order: the 10-cent coin is smaller than the 5-cent coin, and the €1 coin is smaller than the 50-cent coin. That's why the slots in our cards are sized to each coin's actual diameter, not to its value.

## Material and how it's made

The cards are printed in **PLA**, a material that holds its shape, has no smell and is easy to colour. If you'd like to know the difference between PLA, PLA+ and PLA Pro, see our article on [choosing PLA filament](/en/blog/pla-vs-pla-plus-vs-pla-pro-vs-pln/).

Each slot is precisely sized, so the coin presses in with light pressure and **stays put**, with no wobble and no glue. If you're curious how print settings affect strength, see our article on [infill in 3D printing](/en/blog/3d-printing-infill-density-strength/).

If you have your own 3D printer, you can print a coin card yourself. [The model is published on MakerWorld]({{MAKERWORLD_URL}}), with each variant as a separate print profile:

What you get:

- slots sized to the official coin diameters, with a snug fit so the coins stay in place;
- a label for each coin;
- a multicolour (AMS) profile and a single-colour profile with a pause for the filament change;
- printing without supports.

**Print settings:** PLA, 0.2 mm layer height, 15% infill, 3 walls, card printed lying flat. If a coin is too tight or too loose, scale XY by ±0.5%.

Coins are not included. The model contains only the holder, with no reliefs or copies of the designs on the coins themselves.

### Which coins does the coin card hold?

The full set of eight Bulgarian euro coins: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 cents, €1 and €2. Each coin has its own slot.
